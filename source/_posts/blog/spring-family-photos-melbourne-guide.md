Spring weekends fill quickly, so if you are dreaming of golden light and wattle-scented paths, it is worth booking early.

## The best time of day for relaxed family photos

For outdoor sessions, I recommend golden hour, the hour after sunrise or the hour before sunset. Spring makes both options kinder on little bodies.

- **Morning golden hour** works beautifully for early risers and toddlers who are happiest after breakfast. Parks are quieter, the air is crisp, and we can be finished before snack time.
- **Evening golden hour** gives you warm, flattering light and a gentle wind that makes hair and dresses move. In spring, sunset is not too late, so bedtime still feels reasonable.

If naps, sport, or work clash with golden hour, we can still create soft light. I will choose shaded pockets, use backlight, and angle you away from harsh sun. Comfort comes first.

## Outer east locations kids and pets love

Choosing one location keeps walking minimal and energy focused on connection. I look for spots with a short flat walk from the car, open space for play, and a variety of textures like long grass, pines, or sand.

A few family-friendly ideas in Melbourne’s outer east:

- Bushy reserves around Montrose and The Basin with wide trails, gentle slopes, and pockets of dappled light
- Lakeside paths near Lilydale with boardwalks, reeds, and a small beachy edge for bare feet
- Open grass and gums around Croydon and Ringwood where kids can run, climb low logs, and collect leaves
- Yarra Valley edges near Coldstream for soft horizons and fields that glow at sunset

Prefer the coast vibe without a long drive? Some reservoirs and lake reserves give you a beachy look and space to roam, minus the waves. Dogs are welcome at many locations on-lead, and I will confirm local rules when we plan.

I also send a location guide once you are booked in, with more options along the coast and within the Dandenong Ranges. ## Gentle styling without overthinking it

Keep styling simple so you can move, lift, and cuddle.

- Choose 2 to 3 complementary tones across the family, such as sage, oatmeal, and denim; or blush, sand, and charcoal.
- Favour linen, cotton, soft knits, and chiffon that catch the light and allow play.
- Leave neons and big logos at home. They pull attention in bright spring sun.
- Bring a light layer for each person. Spring can flick from warm to breezy quickly.

I will send a short guide before your session and I am happy to review a quick photo of your laid-out outfits. If you want more colour and fabric ideas, my [what to wear for spring family photos](/melbourne-photography-tips/what-to-wear-for-spring-family-photos-in-melbourne/) guide walks through palettes for parks and beaches.

## What to pack for an easy spring session

Keep your bag light so you can be present.

- Water, plus low-mess snacks like crackers or banana
- Wipes, a small towel, and a lint roller
- A spare top for kids and a neutral lead for pups
- A small picnic rug in a muted tone

We will pause for drinks and cuddles as needed. Breaks keep children regulated and often deliver the sweetest in-between moments.

## How I keep sessions fun, calm and unposed

My approach is play-led with gentle prompts. We start with simple movement to shake off camera nerves, like a slow walk holding hands or a game of follow-the-leader. For little ones, I use quick, age-appropriate prompts: counting toes, whispering a silly secret, or finding the softest leaf. For pets, we begin with family cuddles while your dog settles into the new space, then add a short sit-stay for one classic portrait before returning to play.

I read the wind and light and adjust our position so you are comfortable and the light flatters. If a toddler needs a breather, we take it. If a breeze lifts hair, we pivot five steps to shelter. The goal is not performance. It is a memory of how this season feels.
