## The feel of a relaxed couples session

Couples sessions work best when you are moving, talking and interacting. I keep the mood light with simple prompts and give you space to settle into your own rhythm. Expect a blend of candid moments and a few gently guided portraits where I tweak hand placement, posture and light.

We will aim for golden hour, the hour after sunrise or the hour before sunset, for soft, flattering light and less squinting. If we are shooting in late winter or early spring, the sun sits lower and the air is crisp, which can be beautiful for cosy layers and close-up moments.

## Gentle prompts that never feel cheesy

Here are movement-led prompts I use so you never feel stuck:

- **Slow walk and drift:** stroll toward me, brush shoulders, then swap sides.
- **Secret whisper:** lean in, share a made-up secret, and hold the smile.
- **Forehead to forehead:** soft touch, breathe together for three counts.
- **Sway and wrap:** one wraps arms around the other from behind; small side-to-side sway.
- **The spin and settle:** a tiny spin, then land in a cuddle with your chins tucked.
- **Gentle lift:** a small lift or pick-up if comfortable, then settle back into an embrace.

I will adjust angles and backgrounds while you stay in the moment.

## Posing that feels like you

Most couples want guidance without feeling posed. These quick principles help:

- **Connection points:** keep at least two points of contact, like hands, hips or shoulders. It reads as closeness.
- **Soft hands:** relax fingers, avoid flat palms. Slide hands into pockets or hook a thumb into a belt loop.
- **Find a flattering line:** shift your weight onto your back foot, soften knees and angle your bodies slightly toward each other.
- **Chin and posture:** stretch tall through the spine, tip the chin slightly forward and down to avoid shadows.
- **Eyes:** alternate looking at each other, at a nearby point, or toward me. Variety builds a rich gallery.

I will guide micro-adjustments so you never wonder what to do next.

## Melbourne location ideas with heart

Choose a spot that suits your story and the season. Late winter and early spring can offer moody skies, soft light and fewer crowds.

- **Beaches with a breeze:** the Mornington Peninsula and bayside stretches are lovely for windswept hair, dunes and wide horizons. Bring a light layer and embrace the movement.
- **Leafy parks and gardens:** think filtered light, winding paths and textured backdrops. Great if you want greenery without sand.
- **Cobblestones and laneways:** for a slightly urban feel, tuck into quieter side streets near golden hour.
- **At home:** cosy coffee, vinyl playing, making pancakes. In-home sessions are perfect when you want to move slowly, include pets and stay warm.

## What to wear so you can move easily

Comfort comes first. Aim for coordinated tones, not matching outfits. Mid and muted colours photograph beautifully, and movement-friendly fabrics like linen, cotton, soft knits and chiffon add texture. If we are by the coast, bring a layer that catches the breeze. Avoid neon, busy logos and anything that needs constant adjusting. Shoes you can walk in make a difference, especially on sand or uneven paths. Bring a spare layer in a bag you do not mind appearing in a frame, just incase.

## How to prepare and feel comfortable

A little prep goes a long way, especially before cooler spring mornings.

- Choose a meaningful location and commit to one or two spots max to keep travel simple.
- Plan for golden hour. If it looks cloudy, do not worry, cloud softens light beautifully.
- Lay out outfits the day before. Steam or smooth knits, pack a small towel for sandy shoes and a water bottle.
- Eat something light beforehand and bring lip balm and a hair tie if it is breezy.
- Share any must-have shots and the vibe you love. I will create a plan that feels like you.

## Quick FAQ

**What are fun couples photoshoot ideas?** Try a slow coastal walk, coffee at home with music, a picnic in a leafy park, or an urban wander at sunset. Add playful prompts like whispers, spins and tiny lifts.

**How should couples stand in photos?** Angle your bodies slightly toward each other, keep two connection points, relax hands and shift weight onto your back foot. Alternate looking at each other and out to create variety.

**How long should a couple’s photoshoot take?** Typically 60 to 90 minutes. Mini sessions of around 30 minutes are available at select times or as part of wedding bookings.

**How do couples take pictures together without feeling awkward?** Move and talk. Use prompts like walk and drift or forehead to forehead, and let your photographer guide micro-adjustments so you are never guessing.

**How to prepare for a couple photoshoot?** Choose a meaningful location, plan for golden hour, wear comfortable layers in coordinated tones, pack small essentials and share your must-haves with your photographer.
